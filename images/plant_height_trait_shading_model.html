Height as a Trait: Shading, Variance, and Binary Mortality

An individual-plant sketch of Savage-style density dependence: plant performance depends on height, the environment, total stand state, and the surrounding height distribution.

D_i = mean(max(0, h_j - h_i)) / (sigma_h + 1)
P_i = q D_i + (1 - q) S_i
light_i = exp(-s P_i (1 + v CV_h))
hazard_i = m_0 + m_tall h_i^alpha wind_t + m_shade(1 - light_i)

How the Model Works

The simulation starts by generating \(N\) individual plants, each with a height \(h_i\). The spatial-looking stand is only a visualization: the model itself is mean-field, so plant interactions depend on the stand-level height distribution, not on horizontal distance. At each time step that distribution determines light availability, then every living plant is updated in two steps.

Light availability \(L_i\) is the key density-dependent quantity. It is calculated from the trait distribution before the plant grows: a plant loses light when much of the stand is taller than it, and that loss is amplified when the height distribution has high variation.

  1. Calculate a height-growth increment \(\Delta h_i=G_i\), so small plants grow faster and large plants slow down.
  2. Calculate a mortality hazard \(\lambda_i\), then draw a Bernoulli event to decide whether plant \(i\) survives or dies.

The Bernoulli draw is where stochastic binary mortality enters: the plant is either retained in the distribution or removed from it. The full equations and definitions are at the bottom.

\[ D_i = \frac{1}{N_t\max(1,\sqrt{V_h})} \sum_{j=1}^{N_t}\max(0,h_j-h_i), \qquad S_i=\max\left(0,\frac{\bar h-h_i}{\max(1,\sqrt{V_h})}\right) \]
\[ P_i=qD_i+(1-q)S_i, \qquad L_i=\max\left(0.03,\exp\{-sP_i(1+vCV_h)\}\right) \]
\(D_i\): taller-plant pressure, the average amount by which other individuals are taller than plant \(i\).
\(S_i\): below-mean penalty, measuring how far plant \(i\) sits below the current mean height.
\(P_i\): total suppression pressure, with slider \(q\) controlling the weight on \(D_i\) versus \(S_i\).
\(L_i\): light availability after suppression; \(1\) is full light and \(0.03\) is the imposed minimum.

\(D_i\) and \(S_i\) are both trait-based, but they use different levels of approximation. \(D_i\) is the more mechanistic individual-distribution term: it asks how much taller biomass sits above plant \(i\). \(S_i\) is a moment-level approximation: it asks whether plant \(i\) is simply below the canopy center. The slider \(q\) lets you decide how much the model relies on the full individual distribution versus the moment proxy.

\[ \Delta h_i = G_i(h_i,L_i) = 0.34 \frac{1}{1+0.075 h_i^{1.35}} \left[1-\left(\frac{h_i}{42}\right)^{1.65}\right]_+ e^{-0.026 h_i^{1.18}}L_i \]
\[ \lambda_i = 0.0025 +m_{\mathrm{tall}} \left(\frac{h_i}{42}\right)^{2.55} (0.45+W_t^{1.4}) +0.012(1-L_i)^{2.3} \]
\[ \Pr(\mathrm{death}_i)=1-e^{-\lambda_i} \]
Trait state: the stand is the empirical distribution C(h) over individual plant heights.
Density dependence: taller plants reduce light for plants below them in trait space; the penalty increases when height variance creates strong canopy stratification.
Spatial position: the horizontal position is only used for visualization. Competition is non-spatial and depends on the stand-level height distribution.
Mortality: death is a Bernoulli event, with hydraulic and wind costs rising for tall plants and suppression costs rising in deep shade.

Model Sketch

The horizontal coordinate in the drawing is not part of the model. Each individual feels the whole stand-level height distribution.

\[ C(h,t) = \sum_{i=1}^{N_t} \delta(h-h_i(t)) \]
The community state is an empirical trait distribution: one spike at each living plant height.
\[ \bar h = \frac{1}{N_t}\sum_i h_i,\qquad V_h = \frac{1}{N_t}\sum_i (h_i-\bar h)^2,\qquad CV_h=\frac{\sqrt{V_h}}{\bar h} \]
These moments summarize the stand. Mean height gives the canopy center; variance and coefficient of variation describe canopy stratification.
\[ D_i = \frac{1}{N_t\max(1,\sqrt{V_h})} \sum_{j=1}^{N_t}\max(0,h_j-h_i) \]
\(D_i\) is taller-plant pressure. It is kept because shading is asymmetric: short plants are suppressed by taller plants, but tall plants are not equally suppressed by short plants.
\[ S_i=\max\left(0,\frac{\bar h-h_i}{\max(1,\sqrt{V_h})}\right), \qquad P_i=qD_i+(1-q)S_i \]
\(S_i\) is the below-mean penalty. It is kept because it is a simple moment-based proxy for canopy position: a plant below \(\bar h\) is likely to experience less light, even without calculating every pairwise height difference. The slider \(q\) sets the blend: \(q=1\) uses only \(D_i\), while \(q=0\) uses only \(S_i\).
\[ L_i=\max\left(0.03,\exp\{-sP_i(1+vCV_h)\}\right) \]
Light availability declines with suppression \(P_i\). The variance multiplier makes shading stronger when the canopy has a wide height distribution.
\[ G_i = 0.34 \frac{1}{1+0.075 h_i^{1.35}} \left[1-\left(\frac{h_i}{42}\right)^{1.65}\right]_+ e^{-0.026 h_i^{1.18}}L_i \]
This is the deterministic height-growth RHS before stochasticity: height growth slows with size, approaches an architectural upper limit, pays hydraulic cost, and is reduced by light limitation.
\[ h_i^\star = \mathrm{clip}_{[0.25,42]} \left(h_i + G_i(1+0.055\varepsilon_i)\right), \qquad \varepsilon_i\sim\mathcal N(0,1) \]
The model is discrete-time. The height update happens first, with small multiplicative growth noise.
\[ \lambda_i = 0.0025 + m_{\mathrm{tall}} \left(\frac{h_i^\star}{42}\right)^{2.55} (0.45+W_t^{1.4}) +0.012(1-L_i)^{2.3} +0.004\,\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{age}_i>900} \]
Mortality is a hazard, not a smooth biomass loss. Tall plants pay wind/hydraulic risk; shaded plants pay suppression risk.

Exact one-plant RHS used by the simulation:

\[ A_i(t+1)\sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}\left(e^{-\lambda_i}\right), \qquad h_i(t+1)= \begin{cases} h_i^\star, & A_i(t+1)=1,\\ \varnothing, & A_i(t+1)=0. \end{cases} \]
\[ \Pr(\mathrm{death}_i)=1-e^{-\lambda_i} \]
If the plant dies it is removed from \(C(h,t)\). Recruitment then adds new small individuals independently, as a separate immigration/resting-stage-like input.
